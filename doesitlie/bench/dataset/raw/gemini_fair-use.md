# An Exhaustive Legal Analysis of the Fair Use Doctrine (Gemini DR)

The intellectual property regime of the United States is fundamentally rooted in the U.S. Constitution, which grants Congress the power to promote the progress of science and useful arts. (https://www.copyright.gov/title17/title17.pdf)

The basic architectural framework for the current law was established by the comprehensive Copyright Act of 1976. (https://www.copyright.gov/title17/)

Enacted into law on October 19, 1976, as Public Law 94-553 (90 Stat. 2546), this legislation codified the common-law doctrine of fair use into 17 U.S.C. Section 107. (https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section107&num=0&edition=prelim)

The statute has been subject to subsequent amendments, including Public Law 101-650 on December 1, 1990, and Public Law 102-492 on October 24, 1992. (https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section107&num=0&edition=prelim)

Section 107 explicitly declares that the fair use of a copyrighted work is not an infringement of copyright if the work is utilized for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research. (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep510/usrep510569/usrep510569.pdf)

The statute clarifies that teaching purposes can explicitly include the creation of multiple copies for classroom use. (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)

The first statutory factor requires courts to examine the purpose and character of the use, including whether such use is of a commercial nature or is for nonprofit educational purposes. (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)

Transformative use examines whether the new work merely supersedes the objects of the original creation, or whether it adds new expression, meaning, or message. (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Andy-Warhol-Found-for-the-Visual-Arts-Inc-v-Goldsmith-143-S-Ct-1258-2023.pdf)

The commercial nature of a secondary work does not automatically preclude a finding of fair use if the use is sufficiently transformative. (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/campbell-acuff-1994.pdf)

The second statutory factor assesses the nature of the copyrighted work itself. (https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section107&num=0&edition=prelim)

Works that are highly creative are located closer to the core of intended copyright protection than works that are primarily factual or functional. (https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2025/02/reuters-ross-court-ruling-ai-copyright-fair-use)

A secondary use may fail the third factor even if it appropriates only a tiny percentage of the original, provided the copied portion constitutes the qualitative "heart" of the original. (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep510/usrep510569/usrep510569.pdf)

The fourth factor analyzes the effect of the secondary use upon the potential market for, or value of, the copyrighted work. (https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section107&num=0&edition=prelim)

Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569, was decided on March 7, 1994. (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep510/usrep510569/usrep510569.pdf) The District Court initially granted summary judgment in favor of 2 Live Crew. (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/) The Supreme Court unanimously reversed the Sixth Circuit. (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/campbell-acuff-1994.pdf) Following remand, the parties settled out of court. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_v._Acuff-Rose_Music,_Inc.)

Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc., 593 U.S. 1, decided on April 5, 2021. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_LLC_v._Oracle_America,_Inc.) Google copied roughly 11,500 lines of code from the Java SE program. (https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/18-956_d18f.pdf) The copied lines constituted only about 0.4 percent of the entire Java SE program. (https://fairuse.stanford.edu/case/google-llc-v-oracle-america-inc/) Justice Clarence Thomas filed a dissenting opinion. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2020/18-956) Justice Amy Coney Barrett took no part in the consideration or decision of the case. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2020/18-956)

Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith, 143 S. Ct. 1258, decided on May 18, 2023. (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Andy-Warhol-Found-for-the-Visual-Arts-Inc-v-Goldsmith-143-S-Ct-1258-2023.pdf) In 1984, Goldsmith's agency licensed a photograph to Vanity Fair for use as an "artist reference." (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Andy-Warhol-Found-for-the-Visual-Arts-Inc-v-Goldsmith-143-S-Ct-1258-2023.pdf) The district court initially granted summary judgment for AWF. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2022/21-869) The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit reversed. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2022/21-869)

Thomson Reuters Enterprise Centre GmbH v. ROSS Intelligence Inc., decided on February 11, 2025. (https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2025/02/reuters-ross-court-ruling-ai-copyright-fair-use) Judge Bibas found that ROSS had directly infringed 2,243 Westlaw headnotes. (https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2025/02/reuters-ross-court-ruling-ai-copyright-fair-use) The court concluded ROSS's use was not transformative. (https://www.ded.uscourts.gov/sites/ded/files/opinions/20-613_5.pdf)

Bartz v. Anthropic PBC, decided by Judge William Alsup on June 23, 2025. (https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/07/fair-use-and-ai-training) Anthropic had utilized a dataset containing over seven million pirated works. (https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/06/anthropic-and-meta-decisions-on-fair-use) The court certified the class action for the piracy claims, defining the class to encompass 482,460 distinct books. (https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/) The total settlement fund was $1.5 billion paid by Anthropic. (https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/) The settlement included 243,397 unique authors and 15,786 unique publishers. (https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/)

Kadrey v. Meta Platforms, Inc., decided June 25, 2025. (https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/07/fair-use-and-ai-training) Judge Chhabria denied the plaintiffs' motion and granted Meta's cross-motion. (https://www.bakerlaw.com/kadrey-v-meta/)

In re OpenAI: On October 27, 2025, Judge Sidney Stein denied OpenAI's motion to dismiss the output-based copyright infringement claims. (https://www.authorsalliance.org/2025/11/10/copyright-winter-is-coming-to-wikipedia/) OpenAI had intentionally deleted its "Books1" and "Books2" training datasets. (https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/client-alerts/ip-hot-topic-privilege-preserved-openai-escapes-forced-disclosure-of-attorney-communications-in-major-copyright-fight/)

Andersen v. Stability AI: In August 2025, Judge William Orrick issued an order allowing the plaintiffs' core direct infringement claims to move forward. (https://copyrightalliance.org/andersen-v-stability-ai-copyright-case/) A jury trial is scheduled for April 5, 2027. (https://www.bakerlaw.com/services/artificial-intelligence-ai/case-tracker-artificial-intelligence-copyrights-and-class-actions/)

Concord Music Group v. Anthropic: On March 25, 2025, U.S. District Judge Eumi K. Lee denied the application for an interim injunction. (https://www.haldanes.com/publications/legal-update-concord-music-group-v-anthropic-is-it-really-a-victory-for-ai-firms/) The court is scheduled to hear summary judgment motions on July 15, 2026. (https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/02/28/summary-judgment-trial-gets-pushed-back-in-concord-music-v-anthropic-next-fair-use-decision-in-ai-training-might-not-be-until-fall-2026/)

Getty Images v. Stability AI Limited, handed down November 4, 2025 by the High Court of Justice in the UK. (https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Getty-Images-v-Stability-AI.pdf) Getty relied on 11 sample works to prove subsistence and ownership. (https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/getty-images-v-stability-ai-the-most-important-ai-legal-decision-to-date/)

The U.S. Copyright Office Part 1 report was published on July 31, 2024. (https://www.copyright.gov/ai/) Part 3 pre-publication version was released on May 9, 2025. (https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)

On March 20, 2026, the Trump Administration released its National AI Legislative Framework. (https://www.thefirewall-blog.com/2026/04/the-white-house-ai-framework-for-fair-use-and-why-the-courts-may-get-there-first/)
