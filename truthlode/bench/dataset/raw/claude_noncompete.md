# Enforcing Non-Compete Agreements in California, New York, and Texas — Plus the Federal FTC Non-Compete Rule (2026 Status)

## Details

### CALIFORNIA

Controlling statutes. The core provision is California Business and Professions Code § 16600, which states: "Except as provided in this chapter, every contract by which anyone is restrained from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is to that extent void." (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=16600.) As amended by AB 1076, § 16600(b)(1) now provides that the section "shall be read broadly, in accordance with Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP (2008) 44 Cal.4th 937, to void the application of any noncompete agreement in an employment context." (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-7/part-2/chapter-1/section-16600/) Section 16600(c) extends the prohibition to contracts where the person being restrained is not a party to the contract. (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-7/part-2/chapter-1/section-16600/)

Statutory exceptions. Section 16601 permits a non-compete on the sale of the goodwill of a business or of an owner's entire ownership interest. (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=7.&title=&part=2.&chapter=1.&article=) Section 16602 permits a non-compete upon dissolution of or dissociation from a partnership. (https://www.tradesecretsandemployeemobility.com/you-may-think-that-all-non-compete-agreements-are-unenforceable-under-california-law-but-you-would-be-wrong) Section 16602.5 permits a non-compete upon dissolution of, or termination of a member's interest in, a limited liability company. (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-7/part-2/chapter-1/section-16602-5/) The California Supreme Court confirmed in Edwards that "[n]oncompetition agreements are invalid under section 16600 in California even if narrowly drawn, unless they fall within the applicable statutory exceptions of sections 16601, 16602, or 16602.5." (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1269578.html)

Leading case — Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP (2008) 44 Cal.4th 937. Raymond Edwards II, a CPA hired as a tax manager by Arthur Andersen in Los Angeles, signed a non-compete barring him for 18 months after departure from performing professional services for any client on whose account he had worked during the prior 18 months. (https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/california-california-supreme-court-invalidates-non-compete-agreement/) The California Supreme Court held the agreement void under § 16600 and rejected the Ninth Circuit's "narrow restraint" exception. (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1269578.html) The Court expressly declined to address the "so-called trade secret exception to section 16600." (https://www.tradesecretslaw.com/2008/08/articles/trade-secrets/the-california-supreme-court-rejects-the-ninth-circuits-narrow-restraint-exception-to-californias-prohibition-on-employee-non-competition-agreements-in-edwards-v-arthur-andersen-llp/)

2023 legislation (effective Jan. 1, 2024). SB 699 added Business and Professions Code § 16600.5, providing that any contract void under the chapter "is unenforceable regardless of where and when the contract was signed." (https://www.quinnemanuel.com/media/3b1nrqhz/client-alert_-new-california-non-compete-legislation.pdf) SB 699 also created a private right of action allowing employees, former employees, and prospective employees to sue for injunctive relief, actual damages, and attorney's fees. (https://www.lcwlegal.com/news/ab-1076-and-sb-699-codify-california-law-significantly-limiting-the-permissibility-of-non-compete-agreements/) AB 1076 codified Edwards in § 16600(b)(1) and added § 16600.1, requiring employers to notify, by February 14, 2024, current employees and former employees employed after January 1, 2022 that their non-compete clauses are void. (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB1076) Failure to provide the notice is an act of unfair competition subject to civil penalties of up to $2,500 per violation. (https://www.grsm.com/insight/ca-employers-required-to-provide-notice-for-void-noncompetes/)

Out-of-state enforcement. In DraftKings Inc. v. Hermalyn, the First Circuit on September 26, 2024 affirmed a preliminary injunction enforcing a Massachusetts-law non-compete against a former DraftKings executive who moved to California to join Fanatics. (https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca1/24-1443/24-1443-2024-09-26.html)

### NEW YORK

No controlling statute; common-law reasonableness. New York has no general statute governing or prohibiting employment non-competes; enforceability is governed by common-law caselaw applying a reasonableness standard. (https://www.ix-legal.com/blog/2023/august/new-york-caselaw-governing-the-enforcement-of-re/)

Leading case — BDO Seidman v. Hirshberg, 93 N.Y.2d 382 (1999). The New York Court of Appeals articulated the "prevailing standard of reasonableness," holding that a restraint is reasonable only if it: (1) is no greater than required to protect the legitimate interest of the employer, (2) does not impose undue hardship on the employee, and (3) is not injurious to the public. (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ny-court-of-appeals/1345805.html) The full citation is BDO Seidman v. Hirshberg, 93 N.Y.2d 382, 690 N.Y.S.2d 854, 712 N.E.2d 1220 (1999). (https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/court-of-appeals/1999/93-n-y-2d-382-0.html) The Court limited cognizable employer interests to protection against misappropriation of trade secrets or confidential customer lists, or protection from competition by an employee whose services are "unique or extraordinary." (https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I99_0082.htm) The Court found the covenant overbroad as to clients Hirshberg never served, but applied partial enforcement to the portion covering clients he had served within an 18-month restriction. (https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/bdo-seidman-v-hirshberg)

2023 ban bill (S3100A) and veto. The New York Legislature passed S3100A in June 2023, which would have banned nearly all non-compete agreements. (https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/step-too-far-governor-hochul-vetoes-new-york-non-compete-ban) Governor Kathy Hochul vetoed the bill on December 22, 2023, stating in her veto memo that she had "long supported limits on non-compete agreements for middle-class and low-wage workers" but objected to the bill's broad scope. (https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/step-too-far-governor-hochul-vetoes-new-york-non-compete-ban) News reports indicated Governor Hochul sought to limit the ban to individuals earning up to $250,000, while sponsors were agreeable to a $300,000 limit. (https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-york-state-set-prohibit-non-compete-agreements)

2024–2026 legislative efforts. On February 10, 2025, Senator Sean Ryan introduced S4641, a narrower bill that would add Labor Law § 191-d, banning non-competes for "covered individuals" but exempting "highly compensated individuals" earning $500,000 or more annually. (https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S4641/amendment/A) The bill caps permissible non-competes at one year and requires payment of salary (garden leave) during the restriction. (https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/blogs/2025/June/New-York-Senate-Passes-Bill-Restricting-Use-of-Non-Compete-Agreements) S4641 passed the Senate on June 9, 2025 by a 40–22 vote and moved to the Assembly Labor Committee, where it had not advanced as of early 2026. (https://fastdemocracy.com/bill-search/ny/2025-2026/bills/NYB00168321/) In April 2026, Senator Michael Gianaris reintroduced a substantively identical bill, S9759. (https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/blogs/2026/April/New-York-Senate-Re-Introduces-Bill-Prohibit-Certain-Non-Compete-Agreements)

Industry-specific rules. New York Labor Law § 202-k (the 2008 "Broadcast Employees Freedom to Work Act") prohibits broadcasting-industry employers from requiring broadcast employees to refrain from obtaining employment after the conclusion of employment. (https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/labor-law/lab-sect-202-k/)

### TEXAS

Controlling statute — Covenants Not to Compete Act, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§ 15.50–15.52. A covenant not to compete is enforceable "if it is ancillary to or part of an otherwise enforceable agreement at the time the agreement is made to the extent that it contains limitations as to time, geographical area, and scope of activity to be restrained that are reasonable." (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-supreme-court/1589532.html) Section 15.52 makes the statutory criteria exclusive, preempting common-law and other bases. (https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2011/2001724.html)

Two-step enforceability inquiry. Courts first determine whether there is an "otherwise enforceable agreement," then whether the covenant is "ancillary to or part of" that agreement. (https://www.littler.com/texas-supreme-court-provides-new-focus-noncompete-contract-enforcement) Texas is one of the few states where judges reform (rather than void) overbroad covenants under § 15.51(c). (https://worldbizmag.com/texas-non-compete-and-non-solicitation-agreements-what-a-dallas-business-law-attorney-says-is-actually-enforceable-in-2026/)

Leading case — Alex Sheshunoff Management Services, L.P. v. Johnson, 209 S.W.3d 644 (Tex. 2006). The Texas Supreme Court held that a unilateral contract can satisfy the Act: a covenant becomes enforceable when the employer performs its promise, even if that promise was illusory when made, modifying the Court's 1994 decision in Light v. Centel Cellular Co. (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-supreme-court/1490027.html)

Leading case — Marsh USA Inc. v. Cook, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011). The Court held that consideration need not "give rise to" the employer's interest in restraining competition; it need only be "reasonably related to" an interest worthy of protection, such as goodwill. (https://www.theenergylawblog.com/2011/08/articles/business/employment-law/texas-employers-will-welcome-the-decision-in-marsh-usa-inc-v-cook/) The Court held that stock options granted to a managing director were reasonably related to Marsh's interest in protecting goodwill. (https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2011/2001724.html)

2025 legislation — SB 1318 (effective Sept. 1, 2025). Signed by Governor Greg Abbott on June 20, 2025, SB 1318 amends §§ 15.50 and 15.52 and adds § 15.501. (https://www.bracewell.com/resources/texas-sb-1318-changes-to-healthcare-non-competes-effective-september-1-2025/) For physician non-competes, it requires a buyout capped at the physician's total annual salary and wages at termination, expiration no later than one year after termination, and a geographic limit of no more than a five-mile radius from the physician's primary practice location. (https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/texas-sb-1318-tightens-physician-non-compete-rules-extends-restrictions-other-healthcare-practitioners) It also makes a physician non-compete void and unenforceable if the physician is involuntarily discharged without "good cause." (https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2025/07/new-healthcare-practitioner-noncompete-provisions-effective-september-1-2025) New § 15.501 extends the buyout cap, one-year duration limit, and five-mile geographic limit to dentists, nurses, and physician assistants. (https://www.tradesecretsandemployeemobility.com/texas-amends-restrictive-covenant-laws-for-healthcare-providers) The law applies only to agreements entered into or renewed on or after September 1, 2025. (https://www.laboremploymentlawblog.com/2025/07/articles/employment-agreements/texas-enacts-massive-reforms-to-healthcare-provider-non-competes/)

### FEDERAL — THE FTC NON-COMPETE RULE (16 CFR Part 910)

The rule. On April 23, 2024, the FTC voted 3–2 to issue the Non-Compete Clause Rule (16 CFR Part 910), declaring it an unfair method of competition under Section 5 of the FTC Act to enter into or enforce non-competes with workers. (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes) It banned all new non-competes and rendered existing non-competes unenforceable after the effective date — except that existing non-competes with "senior executives" (defined as workers in a policy-making position who received compensation of at least $151,164 in the preceding year) could remain in force. (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes) The FTC stated that "[a]n estimated 30 million workers—nearly one in five Americans—are subject to a noncompete." (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes) The FTC also projected that the rule would foster "more than 8,500 new startups that would be created a year once noncompetes are banned." (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes)

Publication and effective date. The rule was published in the Federal Register on May 7, 2024 (89 FR 38342), with an effective date of September 4, 2024. (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/07/2024-09171/non-compete-clause-rule)

Litigation — Ryan LLC v. FTC. On August 20, 2024, U.S. District Judge Ada Brown granted summary judgment to the plaintiffs in Ryan LLC v. FTC, No. 3:24-cv-00986, holding that the FTC lacks statutory authority to promulgate substantive unfair-methods-of-competition rules. (https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/northern-district-of-texas-permanently-blocks-ftcs-ban-on-non-competes) Judge Brown wrote that "[t]he FTC lacks substantive rulemaking authority with respect to unfair methods of competition." (https://www.npr.org/2024/08/21/g-s1-18376/federal-judge-tosses-ftc-noncompetes-ban) She held the ruling has "nationwide effect," setting aside the rule so it "shall not be enforced or otherwise take effect on September 4, 2024, or thereafter." (https://hensonefron.com/ftc-non-compete-rule-overturned-what-employers-need-to-know-now/)

Litigation — ATS Tree Services v. FTC. On July 23, 2024, U.S. District Judge Kelley Brisbon Hodge denied ATS Tree Services' motion to stay the effective date and preliminarily enjoin the rule, holding that the FTC acted within its authority under the FTC Act. (https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-avoids-preliminary-injunction-non-compete-ban) ATS voluntarily dismissed its case on October 4, 2024. (https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/pennsylvania-company-drops-challenge-to-ftc-noncompete-ban-after-injunction-denial/)

Other litigation. In Properties of the Villages, Inc. v. FTC, Judge Timothy Corrigan granted a preliminary injunction (limited to the named plaintiff) on August 14, 2024, invoking the major questions doctrine. (https://www.rumberger.com/insights/ramifications-of-ryan-the-future-of-the-ftcs-final-rule-banning-non-competes/)

Appeals and abandonment. On October 18, 2024, the FTC filed a notice of appeal of the Ryan ruling to the Fifth Circuit (No. 24-10951). (https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/241022-ftc-appeals-texas-court-s-ruling) On September 5, 2025, the FTC voted 3–1 to dismiss both appeals and accede to the vacatur of the Non-Compete Rule. (https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/insights/publications/rule-abandoned-crackdown-continues-the-federal-trade-commissions-new-non-compete-strategy/)

Formal removal from the CFR. The FTC published a final rule in the Federal Register, effective February 12, 2026, formally removing the Non-Compete Rule (16 CFR Part 910) from the Code of Federal Regulations. (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/12/2026-02866/revision-of-the-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-of-the-cars-rule-removal-of-the-non-compete-rule-to)

Case-by-case enforcement. In a 2025 action against Gateway Services, a pet cremation company, the FTC announced on September 5, 2025 a proposed consent order that "will free nearly 1,800 employees from these restrictive agreements," and finalized the consent order on November 25, 2025 by a 2–0 vote. (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-takes-action-protect-workers-noncompete-agreements)
