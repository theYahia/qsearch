# The Doctrine of Qualified Immunity in Section 1983 Litigation (Gemini DR)

The contemporary framework was forged in Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), an 8-1 decision authored by Justice Lewis Powell, with Chief Justice Warren Burger authoring the sole dissent. (https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/supreme-court-case-library/harlow-v-fitzgerald) The case centered on A. Ernest Fitzgerald, a management analyst for the United States Air Force dismissed after testifying about cost overruns in the C-5A transport plane. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/1981/80-945) The aides moved for summary judgment claiming absolute and qualified immunity. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/1981/80-945) The Court held that "bare allegations of malice should not suffice to subject government officials either to the costs of trial or to the burdens of broad-reaching discovery." (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/603/23-939/)

Saucier v. Katz, 533 U.S. 194 (2001), mandated a rigid two-step sequence for adjudicating qualified immunity defenses. (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/555/223/) The case involved animal rights protester Elliot Katz, who filed a Bivens action against military police officer Donald Saucier. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saucier_v._Katz) The Ninth Circuit had denied Saucier's motion for summary judgment. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2000/99-1977) The majority opinion was authored by Justice Anthony Kennedy. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2000/99-1977)

Pearson v. Callahan, 555 U.S. 223 (2009). (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/555/223/) Police officers had entered a suspect's home without a warrant relying on the "consent-once-removed" doctrine. (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/555/223/) The Court held that the two-step sequence should no longer be regarded as mandatory. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_v._Callahan)

Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730 (2002). (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/730/) Larry Hope was handcuffed to a stationary hitching post in the sun for seven hours. (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/730/) The Court held that "general statements of the law... may apply with obvious clarity to the specific conduct in question." (https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/21-30754/21-30754-2025-12-18.html)

Taylor v. Riojas, 141 S. Ct. 52 (2020), a per curiam decision. (https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/22/22-223/237420/20220908155413004_42769%20pdf%20Forbath%20br.pdf) The Court reversed a Fifth Circuit grant of immunity to officials who confined an inmate in a cell covered in feces for six days. (https://law.utexas.edu/faculty/uploads/publication_files/reading-taylors-tea-leaves--the-future-of-qualified-immunity.pdf) The Court held the conduct was so egregious that any reasonable officer should have realized it offended the Eighth Amendment. (https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/21-30754/21-30754-2025-12-18.html)

McCoy v. Alamu, 141 S. Ct. 1364 (2021), summarily vacated a Fifth Circuit decision granting immunity to a correctional officer who deployed pepper spray into an inmate's face. (https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/21/21-778/200849/20211122122019874_Ramirez%20Cert%20Petition.pdf)

City of Tahlequah v. Bond, 142 S. Ct. 9 (2021), and Rivas-Villegas v. Cortesluna, 142 S. Ct. 4 (2021), reversed appellate decisions denying immunity. (https://law.utexas.edu/faculty/uploads/publication_files/reading-taylors-tea-leaves--the-future-of-qualified-immunity.pdf) In Rivas-Villegas, the Supreme Court held the prior case law was "materially distinguishable." (https://www.cato.org/blog/supreme-court-reaffirms-unwillingness-reconsider-qualified-immunity)

In Ramirez v. Guadarrama, 3 F.4th 129 (5th Cir. 2021), Arlington police tased a suicidal man holding a gas can, causing him to burst into flames. (https://www.cato.org/legal-briefs/ramirez-v-guadarrama-0) The Supreme Court denied certiorari. (https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/21-778_3314.pdf)

In Zorn v. Linton (2025), the incident stemmed from the January 8, 2015 inauguration of Governor Peter Shumlin at the Vermont state capitol. (https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-297_bqm2.pdf) Sergeant Jacob Zorn applied a "wristlock" maneuver to Shela Linton. (https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-297_bqm2.pdf) The Supreme Court reversed in a per curiam opinion. (https://www.dorsey.com/newsresources/publications/client-alerts/2026/3/march-23-supreme-court-update) Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson issued a dissent. (https://www.dorsey.com/newsresources/publications/client-alerts/2026/3/march-23-supreme-court-update)

In Smith v. Scott (2026), officers utilized a bodyweight compression technique on a hallucinating schizophrenic man. (https://ballsandstrikes.org/scotus/smith-v-scott-supreme-court-qualified-immunity/) The Supreme Court summarily vacated the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remanded in light of Zorn v. Linton. (https://nationalpolice.org/u-s-supreme-court-acts-in-accordance-with-the-national-police-associations-amicus-brief-vacating-ninth-circuit-decision-and-delivering-relief-to-law-enforcement/)

Nieves v. Bartlett, 587 U.S. 391 (2019), held that objective probable cause for an arrest generally defeats a First Amendment retaliatory arrest claim. (https://ballotpedia.org/Gonzalez_v._Trevino)

Gonzalez v. Trevino, 602 U.S. ___ (2024). (https://ballotpedia.org/Gonzalez_v._Trevino) Sylvia Gonzalez, a 72-year-old city councilwoman in Castle Hills, Texas, initiated a petition to remove the city manager. (https://www.tpr.org/government-politics/2024-03-20/gonzalez-v-trevino-castle-hills-retaliation-case-goes-before-the-us-supreme-court) The Supreme Court vacated the Fifth Circuit's judgment in a per curiam opinion. (https://ballotpedia.org/Gonzalez_v._Trevino)

Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650 (2014). (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/572/650/) Bellaire police Sergeant Jeffrey Cotton fired three bullets at Robert Tolan. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolan_v._Cotton) The Court ruled that a court must take all facts in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolan_v._Cotton)

Perttu v. Richards (June 18, 2025). (https://ballotpedia.org/Perttu_v._Richards) In a 5-4 decision authored by Chief Justice John Roberts, the Court affirmed the Sixth Circuit's judgment. (https://www.aclu.org/cases/perttu-v-richards) Justice Amy Coney Barrett authored a dissent. (https://www.oyez.org/cases/2024/23-1324)

The New Mexico Civil Rights Act (2021) caps damages at $2 million per claim. (https://www.police1.com/legal/qualified-immunity-a-state-by-state-review) New York City Council passed a local ordinance in 2021 eliminating the qualified immunity defense for NYPD officers. (https://www.police1.com/legal/qualified-immunity-a-state-by-state-review)

In 2022, the Nevada Supreme Court ruled in Mack v. Williams that federal qualified immunity would not apply to state-level actions. (https://www.police1.com/legal/qualified-immunity-a-state-by-state-review) The California Supreme Court ruled in Leon v. County of Riverside (2023). (https://www.police1.com/legal/qualified-immunity-a-state-by-state-review)

The Ohio ballot initiative targets placement on the November 3, 2026 ballot. (https://ballotpedia.org/Ohio_Right_to_Civil_Action_Against_Government_Actors_and_Prohibit_Immunity_Defenses_for_Constitutional_Violations_Initiative_(2026)) On April 22, 2025, the full Supreme Court lifted the stay over the dissents of Justices Kavanaugh, Thomas, and Alito. (https://www.courthousenews.com/supreme-court-clears-way-for-ohio-qualified-immunity-ballot-initiative/)
